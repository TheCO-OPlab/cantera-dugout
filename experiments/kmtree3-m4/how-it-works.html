How this version plays

A match is six moments. In each one somebody attacks, and you make one decision after another until the attack ends. Every decision is a duel between one of your players and one of theirs, and the card tells you both numbers before you choose.

What happens after you choose

The page plays the result in the same order every time, in about three seconds, and all of it on the pitch: the card you chose stays marked beside the pitch; over the two players in the duel a small box shows the two dice rolling next to their names; the verdict says who won the roll and by how much ("Oyarzabal won by 7", "Álvarez won by 2", "Level: nobody won it cleanly"; when a half win and a clean win end the same way, just "Yamal won by 2"), with every plus and minus that applied under it; the ball moves on the field; the headline says what it means; then the next decision appears. The counter at the top says where you are: "Moment 2 of 6, decision 3", because one moment can take a few decisions. When a new moment starts, the last result is gone from the pitch; when the same play goes on, it fades after a beat. A goal says GOAL on the pitch itself. A click or a key skips to the end. On a phone the page first goes back to the top, so the score, the field and the result are on screen.

Settings has "Card detail", with three choices. Lean is the default at every size: one line and each result as an icon with its chance; point at the card (or tap it once on a phone) for the rest. Full is "Show all the working". Short (or opening the page with ?playtesting=yes) shows each option's results in a few words with their chances ("Goal 72% · Saved, Yamal follows up 28%") and any edge a result carries; the rest is under Details on each card. An edge your last decision won shows as a chip on the card it helps ("+2"); point at it for the reason. "+2 in your next duel" means your player adds 2 to his number in the next duel.

A new play says so. The minute at the top runs from the last moment's to this one's while the play before it runs (the first play is the kick-off at 0'), and the commentator opens with "A new attack of yours" (or "of theirs"), and "Yamal has the ball again" when the man on the ball is the one who had it when the last play ended.

What the icons on each result mean

Every result on every card starts with one icon, and so do the headline of the result and each line of "What has happened". The icon is always from your side: your goal is at the bottom of the pitch. Green is good for you, red is bad, grey is neither, and the shape says what happens, so the colour is never the only clue. The one shape that comes in two colours is the arrow back: red when the ball goes toward your goal with them, grey when you pass it back and keep it. The "?" over the cards opens the key; Settings always has it.

Which icon a result gets is worked out once, from what the result does to the ball (where it goes and whose it is, or how play stops), never card by card.

The pitch, and four zones on it

The match is drawn on a pitch standing up: your goal at the bottom, theirs at the top, all 22 players as dots with their shirt numbers (each team in its own kit colours, which the title screen names; the keepers in light grey or yellow). Between moments the match plays itself for about five seconds, like fast forward: passes, runs, now and then a tackle. It stops exactly where the next moment starts, with a ring and a name on the player who has the ball and the zone lit up. "Skip to the moment" jumps to the stop, and 1x or 2x sets the speed. After you choose, the dots do what the result says: the ball goes to the player the result names, a defender who was beaten is left behind, and a lost ball changes side. The play between moments is only a picture: it never changes what happens in the match.

Your attack moves up the pitch one zone at a time. Win a duel and the ball goes forward; lose it and their team attacks from where you lost it. You get at most four decisions in one attack (three if the attack started with you winning the ball back), then their players are all back and the attack is over.

Their attack comes the other way: midfield, the edge of your box, your box. There you choose how to stop them, and your keeper usually has the last decision.

Each decision is a short menu

You see three options you can pick (two when only two make sense) and at most one greyed out. The three are always: the option least likely to lose the ball, the strongest option, and a third that is either something only your players can do or the option that plays most differently from the other two.

Why an option is greyed out:

  • An option your players unlock, and the placed shot, say why in one line under the card: it goes wrong N times in 36, or another option on the menu does at least as well on every count. That other option is always one you can see on the same menu.
  • Any other greyed option cannot come off, goes wrong nearly every time, or is far worse than an option next to it that is no riskier. It has no line of its own: turn on the maths switch in Settings, and the reason is at the end of that card's working.

Then: González is out of position (+2 in your next duel against him).

Card text is shortened here; in the match every part of every number is on the card.

Edges: what one decision wins carries into the next

Beat a defender and he is out of position for the next duel against him. Get the ball to a man who is unmarked and his next duel is easier. The card says so before you choose, in a line that starts with "Then:". Only the biggest edge from before counts on any one duel; a bad one (a booked defender, a man who just missed) always counts.

Keywords and pairs: your squad changes the menu

Some players have a keyword (Dribbler, Crosser, Free-kick taker, Ball winner, Destroyer, Poacher, Shot blocker, Sweeper keeper, Cross catcher, Target man, Late runner, Playmaker) and some pairs play together (One-two, Overlap partners, Strike partners, Pressing pair, Practised routine, Playing out together). Each one adds an option that does not exist without them, marked "Unlocked by". Their players have keywords too: their Free-kick taker makes a foul near your box a shot at goal, and your keyword option that answers one of theirs says "Your answer to". An option your players unlock is only offered when it is a real choice: if it would lose the ball half the time, or another option on the menu does better on every count, it is shown greyed out with that reason.

Four you may not have met yet:

  • Destroyer: once a match he can stop their attack with a foul and the referee shows no card. Near your box it is still a free kick.
  • Sweeper keeper: your keeper can run out of his goal for a ball over your defence or for their Playmaker's pass, and can run out of the penalty area with the ball when they press him.
  • Practised routine (a Free-kick taker and a Target man): at a free kick or a corner, the taker aims at the far post, where the Target man arrives unmarked.
  • Playing out together (a Sweeper keeper and a Playmaker in defence): when they press your keeper, he passes short to the Playmaker, who passes it past their forwards, so your attack starts in midfield.

Counterplay: they learn what you keep doing

  • A defender you beat with a move is +2 against that same move next time, up to +6. The card says it in red: "Tagliafico has seen this run at him twice: +4 to him".
  • A defender who has learned a move stays close to the man on that card, so a different move on the same menu has more room (+1, in gold).
  • Their keeper is +2 against the same kind of shot as your last one (hard or placed).
  • A pass backwards is not learned: it beats nobody.

None of this looks at the score. The switch in Settings changes it to Fade (a move that worked is -2 for a while), Adapt (at half-time they put a second man on your best side) or Off.

The dice

Both players add their stat, the parts on the card, and one roll of a six-sided dice each.

you = your stat + parts + roll(1 to 6) them = their stat + parts + roll(1 to 6) you - them is 4 or more it comes off cleanly you - them is 0 to 3 it half comes off you - them is below 0 it goes wrong

What "half comes off" means is on each result line: a shot saved, a pass that arrives but a step behind the man. When your man wins the roll by 1 to 3, the result banner's verdict says so ("Cubarsí edged it by 3: half a win (4 or more is a clean win)"), so a won roll that only half comes off is never a surprise. The two dice can swing the result by at most 5 either way, so a lead of 9 or more before the dice cannot go wrong, and a deficit of 6 or more cannot come off (that option is shown greyed out).

Shots

  • A shot from inside the box is easier: +3 for your hard shot and for any shot of theirs, +2 for your placed shot.
  • No shot is a sure goal, yours or theirs: the keeper always keeps at least one chance in 4, and a defender throwing himself in front of it when the keeper is out of his goal has at least one chance in 4 too.
  • A placed shot the keeper saves, or one that goes wide, ends your attack, but their team does not get the ball from it. A hard shot the keeper pushes out can fall to one of your players; one he holds starts their attack.
  • The placed shot's card says the keeper has a better chance only when it really scores less often than the hard shot next to it. When it is no better on any count, it is greyed out.

Stamina

A gamble costs the line of the man who tries it: 16 stamina for running and chasing, 5 for anything else. The careful pass, a substitution and running the clock cost nothing. A tired line plays below its numbers, and a substitution brings it back to 100.

Your keeper with the ball

When they press your keeper, he can always kick it out of play: finding the touchline goes wrong at most 3 times in 36 (the card shows it as a part). A throw-in near his own box still ends their attack.

The final

The real 2026 final was level after 90 minutes and went to extra time. This prototype stops at 90: a level final says so on the full-time screen.
